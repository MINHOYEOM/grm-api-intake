FDA Inspection Violations were observed and documented during an inspection of your drug manufacturing facility, USV Private Limited, FDA Establishment Identifier (FEI) 3004086192, at H-13, 16, 16a, 17, 18, 19, 20, 21 & E-22, OIDC Mahatma Gandhi Udyog Nagar, Dabhel, Daman, Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Dam, from April 7 to 17, 2026. This inspection was conducted under FDA’s statutory authority and public health responsibilities to protect the public from unsafe, ineffective, and poor quality drugs. This warning letter summarizes significant violations of Current Good Manufacturing Practice (CGMP) regulations for finished pharmaceuticals. See Title 21 Code of Federal Regulations (CFR), parts 210 and 211 (21 CFR parts 210 and 211). Because your methods, facilities, or controls for manufacturing, processing, packing, or holding do not conform to CGMP, your drug products are adulterated within the meaning of section 501(a)(2)(B) of the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act (FD&C Act), 21 U.S.C. 351(a)(2)(B). We reviewed your May 8, 2026, response to our Form FDA 483 in detail and acknowledge receipt of your subsequent correspondence. Violations of the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act The following are violations identified during our inspection and review. As a reminder, this is not an all-inclusive list of violations at your facility. 1. Your firm failed to establish and follow appropriate written procedures that are designed to prevent microbiological contamination of drug products purporting to be sterile, and that include validation of all aseptic and sterilization processes (21 CFR 211.113(b)). Your firm manufactures aseptically filled parenteral (b)(4) drug products. You failed to implement appropriate processes and systems to assure an ongoing state of control of your aseptic processing operations. You aseptically manufacture injectable products using a (b)(4) approach, with (b)(4) . In addition, your (b)(4) filling line included (b)(4) and was intended as a restricted access barrier system (RABS) but had significant design deficiencies. Your airflow visualization studies (i.e., smoke studies) performed for the ISO 5 vial filling line did not exhibit unidirectional airflow throughout the ISO 5 area. This lack of unidirectional airflow poses a significant risk to drug product sterility. For example, airflow was observed to swirl and move upward. Additionally, your smoke studies did not adequately evaluate airflow at ISO 5 to ISO 7 transition interfaces. We also observed poor aseptic technique during (b)(4) interventions on your (b)(4) filling lines. For example, operators breached first air by extending their forearms over sterile equipment. Additionally, there was inadequate control of material flow from ISO 7 to ISO 5 areas. In your response, you acknowledge deficiencies in your aseptic practices and airflow visualization studies. You add that you engaged an independent consultant to advise on these areas. However, your retrospective risk assessment concluded that the process was in a state of control, citing “no growth” (b)(4) monitoring and compliant environmental monitoring for the (b)(4) filling line. You also cite compliant sterility and bacterial endotoxin results for the vial filling line, and passing media fills for (b)(4) lines. Your response is inadequate. The aseptic processing operations have fundamental flaws in design leading to unidirectional airflow deficiencies, poor ergonomics, and deficient aseptic practices. Such inherent flaws cannot be validated and necessitate redesign. While you engaged consultants for specific aspects of your process design and smoke study program, you lack sufficient commitment to comprehensively remediate your aseptic processing operations, supported by a complete assessment of your aseptic processing operation and equipment replacement. Your risk assessment relies on proxy indicators such as sterility results, media fill pass rates, and environmental monitoring data without systematically addressing deficiencies in the design and control of your operation. The ISO 5 area is critical because sterile products are exposed and therefore vulnerable to contamination. Your aseptic manufacturing process should be designed, and operations executed to prevent contamination hazards to your sterile product. In your response to this letter, provide: Your systematic plan to assure adherence to appropriate aseptic practices, cleanroom behavior, and written procedures, including but not limited to an independent assessment of the following with accompanying corrective action and preventive action (CAPA): o Suitability of actual practices based on extensive retrospective review and prospective observation of aseptic processing operations o Deficiencies in production management oversight, and identification of specific improvements to ensure effective and routine supervisory oversight for all batches o Frequency and depth of quality unit (QU) oversight (e.g., audit, ad hoc, daily interactions) of aseptic processing and its support operations o Adequacy of written procedures and operational ergonomics o The assessment should also evaluate how poor aseptic technique and cleanroom behavior may have affected the quality and sterility of your drugs Describe whether all personnel performing activities that take place in ISO 5 areas are held to ISO 5 personnel monitoring standards 2. Your firm failed to establish laboratory controls that include scientifically sound and appropriate specifications, standards, sampling plans, and test procedures designed to assure that components, drug product containers, closures, in-process materials, labeling, and drug products conform to appropriate standards of identity, strength, quality, and purity (21 CFR 211.160(b)). Your visual inspection program was inadequate to assure your drug products were essentially free of visible particulates. Lack of Alert and Action Limits You lacked action limits for visible particulates. You also only defined alert limits for certain particulate types in selected presentations of your (b)(4) injection drug products. Your visual inspection procedure requires the investigation of atypical trends, yet you failed to investigate atypical trends in (b)(4) injection batch (b)(4) . Without well-defined metrics to characterize acceptable process performance, including but not limited to alert and action limits, it is difficult to reliably detect when a process has deviated from a state of control. Lack of Extrinsic and Intrinsic Particulate Classification You did not classify particulates as extrinsic or intrinsic. However, your procedures state that extrinsic particulates indicating filth, sterility assurance issues, or CGMP violations are unacceptable irrespective of statistical sampling acceptance criteria. Your failure to classify extrinsic particles from your batches can lead to not detecting critical quality and safety risks. Prior to March 28, 2026, you also released all batches of (b)(4) without adequate visible particulate assessment. In your response, you commit to establish alert and action limits for all products. You also commit to implement intrinsic and extrinsic particulate classifications, and a destructive testing program for (b)(4) containers (such as those used for (b)(4) . You state that retrospective destructive testing of (b)(4) batches within expiry did not detect any particles. Your response is inadequate. It does not address your failure to follow your own procedures on batch disposition criteria for extrinsic particles for products already distributed. Moreover, retrospective destructive testing and assessments of flawed visual inspection data cannot substitute for the required characterization and control you lacked at the time of production. Quality Systems Your firm’s quality systems are inadequate. For guidance on establishing and maintaining CGMP-compliant quality systems, see FDA guidances: Q8(R2) Pharmaceutical Development at https://www.fda.gov/media/71535/download, Q9(R1) Quality Risk Management at https://www.fda.gov/media/167721/download, and Q10 Pharmaceutical Quality System at https://www.fda.gov/media/71553/download. Significant findings in this letter demonstrate that your firm does not operate an effective quality system in accord with CGMP. In addition to the lack of effective management oversight of your production operations, we found your QU is not enabled to exercise proper authority and/or has insufficiently implemented its responsibilities. Executive management should immediately and comprehensively assess your company’s global manufacturing operations to ensure that your systems, processes, and products conform to FDA requirements. Drug Production Suspended We acknowledge your initial commitment to suspend production of your (b)(4) drugs at this facility for the U.S. market. You subsequently notified us of your decision to discontinue commercial manufacturing of product from the (b)(4) filling line to the U.S. market. In response to this letter, clarify whether you intend to resume manufacturing (b)(4) drugs for the U.S. market at this facility in the future. If you plan to resume any manufacturing operations regulated under the FD&C Act, notify this office before resuming your drug manufacturing operations. If you resume CGMP activities, you are responsible for resolving all deficiencies and systemic flaws to ensure your firm is capable of ongoing CGMP compliance. In addition, based upon the nature of the violations we identified at your firm, you should engage a consultant qualified as set forth in 21 CFR 211.34 to assist your firm in meeting CGMP requirements. The qualified consultant should also perform a comprehensive six-system audit 1 of your entire operation for CGMP compliance and evaluate the completion and efficacy of all corrective action and preventive action, before you pursue resolution of your firm’s compliance status with FDA. Your use of a consultant does not relieve your firm’s obligation to comply with CGMP. Your firm’s executive management remains responsible for resolving all deficiencies and systemic flaws to ensure ongoing CGMP compliance. In your notification to the Agency, provide a summary of your remediations to demonstrate that you have appropriately completed all CAPA. Conclusion You are responsible for investigating and determining the root causes of any violations and implementing corrective and preventative measures to ensure future and sustained compliance so that these violations and any others do not occur.
